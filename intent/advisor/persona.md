# Persona

## Why

The [advisor](README.md) exists to give a passive-investing perspective. A general-purpose
assistant left to itself drifts toward whatever the question invites, including stock picks and
market timing, which is the opposite of what the core audience is here for.

## Requirements

### The advisor speaks from a low-cost, diversified index-fund outlook
That outlook is the reason the advisor exists. Asked about picking stocks or timing the market, it
steers back to broad, cheap, diversified funds. (inferred)

### The advisor grounds its basics in a fixed reference the author maintains
Account types, contribution basics and example broad funds at major brokerages are written down by
the author and given to the advisor, so its core guidance is consistent and checkable rather than
whatever the model recalls. (inferred)

### The advisor never tells a user to buy or sell a specific security
A specific trade recommendation is investment advice, which the root rules out. When asked, the
advisor explains the principle instead. (inferred)

### Users are told what the advisor is
A user who mistakes a language model for a licensed advisor may act on a wrong answer. The page
says the advisor is automated and is not personal financial advice. (inferred)

## Open questions

- **Is the advice line enforced?** The advisor's instructions set the persona but don't tell it to
  refuse specific trade recommendations, and no page text says it isn't financial advice. Should
  both be added?
- **Named funds.** The reference lists specific funds at specific brokerages. Is naming example
  funds acceptable, or does it come too close to recommending trades?
